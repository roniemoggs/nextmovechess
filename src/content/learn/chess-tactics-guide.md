Tactics are the dynamic building blocks of chess victory. As Grandmaster Richard Teichmann famously said: *"Chess is 99% tactics."*

Mastering these essential motifs will instantly elevate your tactical awareness and help you spot game-winning moves.

---

## 1. The Pin (Absolute vs. Relative)

A pin occurs when an attacking piece targets an enemy piece that cannot move without exposing a more valuable piece behind it:
- **Absolute Pin**: The piece behind is the King. Moving the pinned piece is illegal.
- **Relative Pin**: The piece behind is the Queen or Rook. Moving is legal, but will result in severe material loss.

> **Winning Technique**: "Attack the pinned piece." Add attackers until the defense runs out of protectors.

---

## 2. The Fork (Double Attack)

A fork occurs when a single piece attacks two or more enemy targets simultaneously.
- **Knight Forks**: Particularly deadly because knights jump over pieces and cannot be blocked.
- **Pawn Forks**: Inexpensive pawns attacking two minor pieces often win decisive material.

---

## 3. The Skewer (Reverse Pin)

Unlike a pin, a skewer attacks the more valuable piece directly in front. When that piece flees, the piece sitting behind it is captured.
- Example: A Rook checks the King along a rank, forcing the King to move aside and exposing an undefended Queen behind it.

---

## 4. Discovered Attack & Discovered Check

A discovered attack happens when moving one piece unleashes an attack from another piece standing behind it.
- **Discovered Check**: Gives your moving piece total freedom to capture or create double threats, because the opponent is forced to respond to the check first.
